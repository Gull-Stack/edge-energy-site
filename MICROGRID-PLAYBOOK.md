# MicroGRID Playbook — The Future-Proof Energy Consultation System

Source: internal sales training v1.0 (received 2026-07-10 via Bryce). This is the
messaging constitution for gomicrogridenergy.com. **Every piece of site work — copy,
page structure, CTAs, forms, FAQ, blog, ads — runs through this playbook.** When a
requested change conflicts with it, flag the conflict before shipping.

## 1. Core positioning

- Most solar companies sell equipment. **MicroGRID provides energy as a service with
  certainty.**
- The problem MicroGRID solves is bigger than solar: **the future availability,
  reliability, and cost of electricity.**
- The homeowner is not buying solar. They are buying: protection from utility
  inflation, protection from grid instability, protection from outages, long-term
  energy certainty, household resilience, and control over an essential service.
- **The conversation never begins with solar. It begins with the future.**

## 2. The macro story (the "why now" every page should be grounded in)

Texas demand is growing faster than homeowners realize (ERCOT forecasted load growth):

1. **AI** — hyperscale AI facilities consume power equal to tens of thousands of homes; Texas is a primary destination.
2. **Data centers** — Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, xAI, colocation.
3. **Population growth** — every new home needs power, transmission, generation.
4. **Electrification** — EVs, heat pumps, home automation; each household uses more than the last generation.
5. **Industrial expansion** — manufacturing, semiconductors, logistics.

The homeowner controls none of it (ERCOT policy, utility decisions, fuel prices,
regulation) — **yet ratepayers pay for all of it**: higher rates, higher delivery
charges, more volatility, reduced peak reliability. The homeowner owns the risk —
unless they do something different.

The future-proofing framing: *"What happens when Texas doubles electrical demand?
Who pays for new generation, transmission, upgrades? Ratepayers. Would you rather
stay fully exposed or lock in a large portion of your household energy today?"*
This shifts the discussion from solar panels to **long-term energy strategy**.

## 3. Philosophy (tone rules for all copy)

- **Never sell fear. Never sell urgency. Never sell solar.**
- Help the customer understand the future → evaluate their exposure → decide whether
  they want protection → then show how MicroGRID solves it.
- People move toward futures **they create**, not futures they are sold. Copy should
  ask and let the reader conclude, not assert and push.
- Discover before presenting. Understand before recommending. Educate before
  enrolling. Protect before selling.

## 4. The D.R.I.V.E.R.S. consultation framework

| Stage | Name | Move |
|---|---|---|
| D | Discover future concerns | "What concerns you most about the future of electricity in Texas?" Listen. Don't interrupt. |
| R | Rate importance | "1 to 10, how important is solving that for your family?" Numbers create honesty. |
| I | Identify existing motivation | **"Why didn't you choose a LOWER number?"** (never "higher" — lower surfaces motivation, higher surfaces objections) |
| V | Visualize the future | "Imagine this is solved five years from now. What's different?" Let them create it. |
| E | Explore personal significance | "Why does that matter?" — repeat until you reach the emotional driver (security, retirement, family). |
| R | Reflect their reasons | Mirror/summarize what they said. Never pitch. "Did I get that right?" Pause. |
| S | Secure the appropriate next step | "What do you think is the next logical step?" The customer chooses (analysis, site assessment, design, spouse review, enrollment). |

Consequence question (after motivation): "What happens if nothing changes?" — the
cost of inaction becomes real because **they** said it, not you.

Discovery areas: cost (bills, inflation, retirement, fixed income), reliability
(outages, weather, grid), control (utility/regulator dependence, predictability),
family (children, elderly, medical, work-from-home), property (past solar
dissatisfaction, contractor failures, home value).

## 5. The offer (what the site may say the program is)

Risk framing: a traditional utility customer carries rate/reliability/inflation/
infrastructure/demand-growth risk. A traditional solar-loan customer carries
ownership/maintenance/obsolescence/replacement/performance risk. **MicroGRID: EDGE
owns the system; the homeowner receives energy as a service.**

Typical program structure (say "typical," don't guarantee):
- No upfront equipment purchase
- Fixed energy rate, no annual escalator
- Full-roof solar design where feasible
- ~80 kWh battery storage
- Significant backup capability
- Ongoing operational support
- Reduced exposure to future utility inflation

## 6. Objection patterns (useful for FAQ copy)

- "I need to think about it" → "What specifically would you like to think through?"
- "I need to talk to my spouse" → "What will matter most to them?" + joint meeting.
- "I'm comparing companies" → most companies sell equipment; MicroGRID sells energy
  certainty. Different products.
- "Solar doesn't work" → "What experience led you to that conclusion?" Discover
  first, educate second.

## 7. COMPLIANCE — hard prohibitions (apply to ALL site copy, verbatim scope)

The site (like reps) may NEVER:
- Guarantee savings
- Guarantee outage protection
- Guarantee utility bill elimination
- Promise tax credits to homeowners
- Misrepresent ownership (EDGE owns the system)
- Misrepresent performance, transferability, or timelines

Audit existing pages against this list whenever touching copy. Use "designed to,"
"typical," "significant backup capability," "reduced exposure" — not "you will save,"
"never lose power," "eliminate your bill."

## 8. What this means for the website, concretely

- **Heroes/headlines** lead with the future of Texas energy and certainty, not
  panels/batteries/price. Equipment is the "how," never the headline.
- **CTAs** frame a customer-chosen next step ("Get Free Design" = a no-pressure
  analysis), never pressure ("Buy now," countdown timers, fear hooks).
- **Page flow** mirrors DRIVERS: raise the future question → let the visitor locate
  their concern (cost/reliability/control/family) → consequences of inaction (in
  their words, e.g. testimonials) → visualize the protected future → present
  MicroGRID as the mechanism → soft, chosen next step.
- **Testimonials/social proof** should express the emotional drivers (security,
  predictability, independence) — and must be genuinely attributable to MicroGRID
  (see 7/9 review cleanup).
- **Blog/SEO content** = the macro story: AI/data-center demand, ERCOT load growth,
  electrification, who pays. Educate; never fear-monger.
- **The one question everything serves:** "As Texas becomes increasingly dependent
  on electricity, and electricity becomes more valuable, more expensive, and more
  critical to daily life, what should my family do today to protect itself?"
